The setup

The vault at 0xC82274b045f7481C953f3DC0e88e9e54B308Dfd4 holds QOR and pays only when a withdrawal carries the guardian's ML-DSA-87 post-quantum signature, verified on chain by QoreChain's pqcVerify precompile. The classical (secp256k1) key alone — the one a quantum computer can derive — pays nothing.

Below, your browser builds the exact transaction an attacker would send: a withdraw of 0.5 QOR to 0x0d95…24FD, signed with a randomly generated ML-DSA-87 key instead of the guardian's. It is executed as a read-only eth_call against the public testnet RPC — no wallet, no gas, no transaction is sent. What the chain answers is what a real attack would get.

The comparison — when the signature is real

A vault that rejects everything proves nothing. Here is the other side, live from the chain — no wallet, no gas, nothing is sent:

Why is there no "pay for real" button? A live payment needs a fresh, valid signature — and if it were printed on this page, anyone could submit it and drain the vault. So the "it pays" side is proven with the signature the guardian already used (verified above) and the receipt of the payment it made (checked above).
